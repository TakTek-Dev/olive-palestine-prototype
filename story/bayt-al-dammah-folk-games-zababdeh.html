HeritageZababdeh, Jenin7 Oct

A Zababdeh centre revives Palestinian folk games, including one built on Sebastia's olive press

Bayt Al-Dammah teaches strategy and movement games and games that recall depopulated villages. Its director wants a catalogue and digital archive.

By the Olive Palestine land desk · based on reporting by PNN, Wednesday 7 October 2026 · 1 min read

Bayt Al-Dammah in Zababdeh was set up to keep Palestinian folk games from disappearing, director Samahear Al-Roumi told PNN.

The games fall into three groups: strategy games such as al-dammah and mancala, movement games such as seven stones and hopscotch, and games that document depopulated towns and villages. An 'Olive Press' game teaches players about Sebastia before they play.

The centre hosts children and mothers on Sunday afternoons. Some visitors over 50 learned for the first time that games they knew were Palestinian. The project was picked from 293 entries at an event in Jordan.
