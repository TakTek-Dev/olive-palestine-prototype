01

The idea

Cut an olive across and you see skin, flesh and a stone. Look down on a terraced hill in Palestine and you see the same drawing: rings of dry-stone walls, crowded where the slope is steep, and the village on the summit.

The hills where Palestine's olives grow were built by hand, wall by wall, over centuries: the terraces of Battir, of Sebastia, of every village around Ramallah and Hebron. The site's subject is that land and what comes from it. So the mark is the fruit and the hill at once: its skin is the outer wall, the walls inside it are terraces, and the stone at its heart is the summit.

The summit is drawn in terra rossa, the red soil the trees stand in. It is the only red in the identity, and it always means the same thing: here, now. It marks today on the season line, the current month in the archive, the section you are in. The rest of the identity is limestone and black olive, the colours of the walls and the ripe fruit.

02

The mark

One outline, drawn three times. Each wall is the olive's own outline drawn smaller and moved up the slope, so the walls close up on the steep face, towards the stalk, and open out on the gentle one.

Outline
An egg: half-length 1, half-width 0.64, fuller by 12% at the stalk end, hung at 30° off its stalk.
Walls
The skin at 1.00, then walls at 0.72 and 0.46. Each moves towards the summit by the share it shrinks.
Weights
The skin is 2.2 times heavier than the walls, so at small sizes the olive holds and the walls fall back.
Summit
A stone of 0.17, always terra rossa on limestone and lifted terra rossa on black olive.
Under 24 px
One wall is dropped: skin, a single terrace and the summit. The favicon uses this drawing.

03

The wordmark

«Olive» in Literata's display cut, «Palestine» in its italic: one name in two voices, the fruit and the place. The mark stands as tall as the capitals, its stalk rising above them like an ascender.

Clear space is the height of the summit stone times four on every side. Smallest horizontal lockup: 140 px wide on screen, 30 mm in print; below that, use the mark alone.

Never recolour the walls, never set the name in another face, never put the summit in any colour but terra rossa (or black olive on terra rossa).

06

The course and the season

Two devices carry the idea through the site. The course is a dry-stone wall face, drawn as the site's rule under every section. The season line is the picking calendar, with today as the summit stone.

The season line is drawn at build time from the ministry's dates. The picked part of the season fills up to today; today's stone is the only red on it.

Pick · Press · Plant

The month on the land is three steps, set like terraces down the page: each step lower than the one before, each with its course under it.

Now

The summit stone at text size marks the live part of any list: the current month, today's date, the section you are in.

The hill

On black grounds the mark's walls are continued downhill as a contour drawing: the same geometry, mapped further out.

07

Motion

The loader lays the walls. The outer wall goes down first, stone by stone from the low side, then each terrace above it, then the stone drops onto the summit and the finished mark moves into the masthead. It plays once per visit, and not at all when the reader asks for less motion.

08

Photographs

People and places in their own colour, with a caption that says where, when and who took it. A story gets a photograph of what it is about, or none at all.

Natural colour

No filters, no duotone. The harvest is green, violet and black; the soil is red. That colour is the subject.

Honest captions

Place, date, photographer, licence. An archive photograph says it is one; a photograph from another year says which year.

Crops

4:3 for the lead, 3:2 for stories, squares for lists. Hands and faces stay in frame.
